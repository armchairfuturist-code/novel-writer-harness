# Worldbuilding Consistency

## Rule-Based Settings

Every fictional world has rules. Once established, these rules are LAW:
- Magic systems: what magic can and cannot do
- Technology level: what technology exists and its limits
- Social systems: hierarchy, customs, taboos
- Physical laws: gravity, weather, geography

## Detection Strategy

When evaluating a chapter:
1. Identify every world-rule referenced in the draft
2. Cross-reference against the established canon
3. If a character bypasses an established limitation without explanation → FATAL
4. If a new world fact is introduced that contradicts a prior fact → FATAL
5. If a new world fact expands on existing lore without contradiction → note but don't flag

## Common Violations

- **Fast travel**: characters crossing impossible distances in the stated time
- **Magic inflation**: spell power growing without in-world justification
- **Technology anachronism**: advanced tech appearing in low-tech settings
- **Social structure collapse**: rigid caste/class systems suddenly treated as flexible without story reason
- **Geography drift**: locations moving relative to each other, climate zones changing

## Spatial Consistency

Track room layouts, building geography, and city maps:
- If a room was described as having one door in Ch 2, it can't have two in Ch 8
- If the tavern is "east of the river" in Ch 1, it stays east
- Temporal-spatial: characters can't be in two places simultaneously
