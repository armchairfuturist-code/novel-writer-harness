# Sensory Immersion Techniques

## The Five Senses Rule

Every scene should engage at least 2-3 senses:
- **Sight**: the most used, the most abused. Be specific ("oak" not "wood")
- **Sound**: the most underused. Silence is also a sound.
- **Touch**: texture, temperature, pressure - grounds the reader in the body
- **Smell**: the most memory-linked sense. Use sparingly for impact.
- **Taste**: rarely used, powerful when deployed

## Show-Don't-Tell Patterns to Avoid

- "She felt sad" becomes "Her throat tightened."
- "He was angry" becomes "His jaw clenched."
- "The room was cold" becomes "Her breath misted in the air."
- "It was terrifying" becomes "Her hands wouldn't stop shaking."

## Sensory Density by Genre

- **Thriller/Horror**: high touch + sound, low taste
- **Romance**: high touch + sight, moderate smell
- **Fantasy/Sci-Fi**: high sight + sound (worldbuilding), moderate touch
- **Literary**: balanced, with emphasis on interiority over external senses
